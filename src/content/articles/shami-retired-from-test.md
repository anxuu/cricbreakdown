Indian cricket has witnessed the end of another remarkable chapter as veteran fast bowler Mohammed Shami announced his retirement from Test cricket on November 6, 2026, bringing an end to his 13-year journey in the longest format of the game.

The announcement came on a rather emotional date for Shami. November 6, 2013, was the day he made his Test debut against the West Indies at Eden Gardens, Kolkata. Exactly 13 years later, he decided to bid farewell to the format that played a significant role in establishing him as one of India's finest fast bowlers.

However, this is not the end of Mohammed Shami's cricketing career. The experienced pacer will continue to remain available for white-ball cricket.

## Mohammed Shami's Early Life: From Sahaspur to Professional Cricket

Born on September 3, 1990, in Sahaspur, a village in Amroha district of Uttar Pradesh, Mohammed Shami developed an interest in cricket at a young age. His father, Tauseef Ali, a former fast bowler, recognised his son's talent and encouraged him to pursue cricket seriously.

Under the guidance of coach Badruddin Siddique, Shami began developing his fast-bowling skills. However, his journey was not easy. After missing out on selection for the Uttar Pradesh Under-19 team, he moved to Kolkata around 2005–06 in search of better cricketing opportunities.

His early days in Kolkata involved financial struggles and playing in local club competitions. Gradually, his performances attracted attention, and he earned opportunities with Bengal in domestic cricket.

His impressive performances in the Ranji Trophy and India A cricket eventually opened the doors to international cricket. What began as a young boy's dream in a small village eventually turned into an opportunity to represent India at the highest level.

## Mohammed Shami's Test Career: Numbers That Define His Legacy

Shami finishes his Test career with an impressive record of 229 wickets in 64 matches at an average of 27.71. His tally includes six five-wicket hauls, with his best bowling figures being 6/56.

These numbers only tell part of the story. Shami was not just a wicket-taking bowler; he was someone who could change the direction of a Test match through a single spell.

## The Art of Seam Movement and Reverse Swing

One of the biggest strengths of Mohammed Shami was his ability to extract movement even when conditions were not particularly favourable for fast bowling.

His upright seam position, natural pace, accuracy and exceptional reverse swing made him a difficult bowler to face, especially with the older ball.

Whether it was the challenging conditions in England, the pace-friendly surfaces of Australia or the unpredictable pitches of South Africa, Shami consistently demonstrated his ability to compete against the best batters in the world.

His partnership with India's pace attack, alongside Jasprit Bumrah and other experienced fast bowlers, played an important role in strengthening India's overseas Test performances.

## Injuries, Comebacks and Unforgettable Performances

Shami's career was never without challenges. Injuries and fitness setbacks interrupted different phases of his journey, but his ability to return and deliver made his career even more special.

His performances in overseas conditions remain among the most memorable moments of his Test career.

The 5/28 spell against South Africa at Johannesburg in 2018 and his ability to trouble established batting line-ups with both the new and old ball demonstrated his importance to India's bowling attack.

His final Test appearance came in the 2023 World Test Championship Final against Australia at The Oval.

Despite continuing to perform in domestic cricket, a return to India's Test setup never materialised.

## Why Did Mohammed Shami Retire From Test Cricket?

Shami's retirement comes after a prolonged absence from India's Test side. His last appearance in the format was in June 2023, and his exclusion from India's immediate Test plans had raised questions about his future.

Interestingly, Shami had continued to demonstrate his bowling abilities in domestic cricket, showing that he still had plenty to offer.

While the exact reasons behind his decision remain personal, his retirement marks the conclusion of a significant chapter in Indian fast-bowling history.

## Mohammed Shami's Retirement Message: A Farewell to the White Jersey

Announcing his decision through social media, Shami shared an emotional message reflecting on his journey, struggles, injuries and memories.

He expressed gratitude towards his teammates, coaches, family, supporters, the BCCI and the Cricket Association of Bengal for standing beside him throughout his career.

His farewell carried a simple but powerful sentiment: he was leaving the white jersey, but not the memories associated with it.

## Is Mohammed Shami Retiring From All Formats?

No. Mohammed Shami has announced his retirement only from Test cricket.

The Indian fast bowler remains available for limited-overs cricket, including ODIs and T20s, along with domestic cricket and the Indian Premier League.

This means cricket fans could still witness Shami producing his trademark seam movement and reverse swing in the shorter formats.

## The Final Verdict: An Era That Deserves to Be Remembered

Mohammed Shami's retirement from Test cricket is undoubtedly an emotional moment for Indian cricket fans, particularly those who appreciate the art of fast bowling and the traditional five-day format.

From his memorable debut at Eden Gardens to delivering crucial spells in overseas conditions, Shami built a career defined by skill, determination and resilience.

He may be leaving the Test arena, but his contribution to India's fast-bowling legacy will remain significant.

229 wickets. 64 Tests. 13 years. Countless memories.

The white jersey will no longer have Mohammed Shami in it, but his name will always remain a part of India's Test cricket story.

Thank you, Mohammed Shami, for the memories. 🇮🇳
