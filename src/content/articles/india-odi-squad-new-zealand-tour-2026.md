India's ODI squads for the New Zealand tour in 2026 have been announced in two groups: one for the first three matches and another for the fourth and fifth ODIs. Shubman Gill leads the opening group, while Shreyas Iyer captains the final two matches.

The selection has raised questions ahead of the 2027 ODI World Cup. New Zealand are a strong ODI side who have consistently performed well in ICC tournaments, and India cannot afford to take this series lightly. The tour is an opportunity to build a reliable combination rather than treat ODI preparation as a secondary priority.

## India’s ODI Squads for New Zealand

For the **first three ODIs**, the squad list is: KL Rahul, Yashasvi Jaiswal, Devdutt Padikkal, Shubman Gill (captain), Rishabh Pant, Dhruv Jurel, Ravindra Jadeja, Nitish Kumar Reddy, Jasprit Bumrah, Mohammed Siraj and Prasidh Krishna.

For the **fourth ODI**, the squad list is: KL Rahul, Yashasvi Jaiswal, Devdutt Padikkal, Shubman Gill (captain), Rishabh Pant, Dhruv Jurel, Ravindra Jadeja, Nitish Kumar Reddy, Jasprit Bumrah, Mohammed Siraj and Prasidh Krishna.

## Is India Prioritising Test Cricket Over ODI Preparation?

India's Test series against New Zealand is important, but the 2027 ODI World Cup is also approaching. With Shubman Gill, KL Rahul and other players expected to focus on Test preparations, India are effectively dividing their attention between formats.

Workload management is understandable, but India need to establish a reliable ODI combination well before the World Cup. New Zealand have consistently challenged India, and this series is an opportunity to identify the right players and give them clarity about their roles.

If India keep changing their plans after every defeat, players may struggle to establish themselves. Experimentation is necessary, but it should have a clear purpose.

## India’s Bowling Attack Raises Concerns Ahead of the New Zealand Tour

Jasprit Bumrah's absence makes India's bowling combination even more important. In the last defeat against the West Indies, India's bowlers conceded too many runs and struggled to remain economical. The team cannot afford similar performances against a strong New Zealand batting lineup.

Mohammed Siraj and Arshdeep Singh will be important options, but the team management must use them effectively, particularly in the death overs. Siraj needs clearly defined responsibilities, while Arshdeep's inclusion provides an opportunity to add variety and strengthen the pace attack.

Gurnoor Brar continues to receive backing, with his height and ability to generate bounce potentially useful in New Zealand. However, he needs to convert that potential into consistent performances. Fitness and control are important concerns, and the selectors must evaluate him on results rather than potential alone. Prasidh Krishna and Anshul Kamboj will also need to make their opportunities count.

The inclusion of Auqib Nabi and Yash Thakur for the final two ODIs gives India additional bowling options to assess. These opportunities should have a clear purpose, with the team identifying which bowlers can handle the new ball, control the middle overs and deliver at the death. India need dependable performers, not just more names in the squad.

## Washington Sundar, Kuldeep Yadav and Ravindra Jadeja: Selection Questions

Washington Sundar's omission is surprising given the backing he has received across formats. Naman Dhir's emergence may have influenced the selectors' plans, as he offers batting and some off-spin. However, a batter who can bowl a few overs is not automatically a dependable all-rounder.

India should avoid making major decisions based on one or two performances. Players need clearly defined roles and reasonable continuity to establish themselves internationally.

Kuldeep Yadav and Ravindra Jadeja also raise questions about India's plans for the 2027 World Cup. Kuldeep remains a talented wrist-spinner, but New Zealand's batters can be comfortable against spin, particularly when they have opportunities to attack. His selection should depend on the conditions and the opposition rather than reputation alone.

Jadeja's absence is equally noteworthy given his experience in major tournaments. With Axar Patel, Kuldeep and Naman Dhir offering different combinations, the selectors appear to be exploring alternatives. India's final choice should consider the balance between batting, bowling and fielding, rather than making decisions based only on recent results.

The bigger concern is consistency in selection. India need to decide what they expect from each player and give them a fair opportunity to fulfil that role.

## India’s Opening and Middle-Order Combination

The squad lists provided here include KL Rahul, Yashasvi Jaiswal, Devdutt Padikkal, Shubman Gill, Rishabh Pant and Dhruv Jurel. This group gives India multiple options across the top and middle order, with Pant and Jurel also offering wicketkeeping choices. The team management will need to define each player's role and give the combination enough continuity to build confidence ahead of the 2027 ODI World Cup.


## India Need More Consistency in Selection

One of the biggest concerns surrounding India's selections is the apparent lack of continuity. Players can receive strong backing and then lose their place after a few disappointing performances, while others are brought in without a clearly defined role.

Experimentation is necessary before a World Cup, but it should be planned rather than driven by short-term reactions. The selectors must identify a stable core, establish the best combination for different conditions and give players enough opportunities to demonstrate their abilities.

The New Zealand series should help India answer important questions about their batting order, bowling options and backup players. It should not become another exercise in changing the plan after every defeat.

## Conclusion

India's ODI squads for New Zealand have raised important questions about Jaiswal's omission, Washington Sundar's absence, Jadeja's future and the continued backing of Gurnoor Brar. At the same time, Arshdeep Singh's inclusion and opportunities for emerging bowlers give India a chance to strengthen their options.

With the 2027 ODI World Cup approaching, India need consistency, clearly defined roles and a reliable core squad. New Zealand will be a serious test, and the selectors must use this series to build a team capable of competing against the strongest sides rather than relying on repeated experiments without a clear direction.
