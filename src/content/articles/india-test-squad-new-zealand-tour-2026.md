India are heading into another overseas Test challenge after getting back on track following the home-series defeat to South Africa, which included a heavy 408-run loss. The conditions in New Zealand will be a different test: Wellington and Christchurch can offer seam movement and assistance for fast bowlers, so India's success will depend on how well the batters handle the new ball and how effectively the pace attack uses the conditions.

The squad has a blend of established performers and less experienced options. Shubman Gill will lead the side, KL Rahul is vice-captain, and Jasprit Bumrah adds quality to the pace group. Rishabh Pant's availability remains subject to fitness clearance.

Let's look at the squad, assess the batting and bowling combinations, and consider the key selection questions before the two-Test series.

## Batting analysis: can India's top order adapt overseas?

Against Afghanistan at home and Sri Lanka away, India's batters showed what they can do in those conditions. New Zealand is a different challenge. If the pitch offers swing and seam, the top order will need patience, strong judgement outside off stump and the ability to leave the ball well.

### Yashasvi Jaiswal: proven ability, but another test awaits

Jaiswal's previous overseas performances give India reason to be optimistic. In the five-Test series in England, he scored two centuries, one in the first Test and another in the final match. Against Australia, he also produced a huge innings of 161 runs.

Those performances show that Jaiswal can make big scores away from home. The challenge in New Zealand will be to negotiate the first spell, avoid giving away his wicket early and turn starts into match-shaping innings.

### Shubman Gill: preparation, form and captaincy

Gill was Player of the Series during India's previous overseas Test tour of England. India will need that quality again, both with the bat and in the way he leads the team.

There have been reports that Gill plans to take four or five personal net bowlers to New Zealand for red-ball preparation and pay their travel and accommodation costs himself. He was also expected to play Punjab's Ranji Trophy match against Chhattisgarh at Mullanpur from 18 October, before travelling to New Zealand around 23 or 24 October. This preparation could help him get used to the conditions, but the real test will come when New Zealand's bowlers challenge him with the new ball.

### KL Rahul and Devdutt Padikkal: reliable options

Rahul and Padikkal were notable performers in India's previous away Test series against Sri Lanka. Padikkal's numbers were exceptional: **328 runs in three innings at an average of 109.33**. That kind of return makes a strong case for giving him a meaningful role in the batting order.

Rahul also brings valuable experience of batting overseas. Between Rahul, Padikkal and Jaiswal, India have players who have already shown they can score runs away from home. They will need to bring that form into conditions where the ball may move for longer periods.

### Dhruv Jurel: a promising middle-order option

Jurel's performance in the previous series was another positive. He scored **173 runs in three innings at an average of 86.50**, a promising return that suggests he can contribute more than just wicketkeeping.

Jurel should be viewed as a genuine option in India's playing XI, not simply as Pant's backup. His 173 runs in three innings at an average of 86.50 make a strong case for his batting contribution, and India could consider playing both wicketkeeper-batters depending on the balance of the XI and Pant's fitness.

### Rishabh Pant: India's highest-ranked Test batter

For me, Pant could be one of India's most important players if he receives fitness clearance. He has a proven ability to change the pace of a Test innings and put pressure on the opposition, including in overseas conditions. His aggressive approach can be especially valuable after the top order has spent time negotiating the seam movement.

In the ICC Men's Test batting rankings referenced for this analysis, **Pant is ranked seventh, Shubman Gill ninth and Yashasvi Jaiswal 11th**. Pant is therefore India's highest-ranked batter among the three. Rankings are updated regularly, so readers should check the latest ICC list for the most recent positions.


## Bowling analysis: should India play three specialist pacers?

My preferred combination would be three specialist fast bowlers, a seam-bowling all-rounder and a spinner. That could give India a pace group built around Bumrah, Siraj and Prasidh Krishna, with Nitish Kumar Reddy as the fourth seam option and Ravindra Jadeja providing spin and batting depth. The final selection will depend on the surface, player fitness and the workload expected from each bowler.

### Jasprit Bumrah: a match-winner, if workload allows

Bumrah's numbers in overseas conditions underline why he is so important. In the five-Test series in England, he played three Tests and took **14 wickets**. Against Australia, he took **32 wickets across five Tests**, an outstanding return.

There is one concern: India need to manage his workload and cannot simply assume he will play every match. His last Test against South Africa was in November 2025, according to the timeline provided for this analysis, so match fitness and rhythm will be important considerations.

The last time India played New Zealand in the 2021 World Test Championship final at Southampton, Bumrah did not take a wicket. That single match does not define his ability, and India will hope he can make a much bigger impact this time. If fit and available, he will be central to India's plans.

### Mohammed Siraj: a workhorse in overseas conditions

Siraj's energy and ability to bowl long spells make him a major option overseas. In the last Test series against Sri Lanka, the figures provided for him were **four wickets in four innings, an economy rate of 2.34 and an average of 25**. The wicket tally was not especially high, but economy and pressure can still matter in Test cricket.

His overseas tours of England and Australia are a stronger example of his wicket-taking ability: he took **43 wickets across 10 matches** against those two sides. Siraj can keep running in, attack the stumps and make batters play. In New Zealand, that discipline will be as important as his aggression.

I expect another strong overseas series from him if he maintains his fitness and control.

### Prasidh Krishna and Nitish Kumar Reddy: important but less experienced

Prasidh offers height and bounce, which could be useful on New Zealand pitches. Nitish gives the team a seam-bowling all-round option and could help balance the XI. Both have less experience than Bumrah and Siraj in carrying the attack through a demanding overseas Test series.

Control will be crucial. If Prasidh and Nitish can keep the scoring rate in check while offering movement, India can maintain pressure without overworking the senior quicks. Nitish's batting also matters because he is the squad's main pace-bowling all-round option.

### Jadeja and the other bowling options

Jadeja may not be India's first-choice wicket-taking option if the conditions strongly favour pace, especially early in a match. Still, his value should not be judged only by wickets. He can control an end, contribute with the bat and become more important as a Test progresses and the surface changes.

Kuldeep Yadav and Washington Sundar give India further spin options. Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj add pace depth, although selecting the right combination will be a balance between experience, current form and the conditions on the day.

## Notable omissions: Sarfaraz Khan and Manav Suthar

### Sarfaraz Khan

Sarfaraz's omission may surprise fans who want another proven domestic run-scorer in the middle order. His selection could have offered another batting option, but the selectors have chosen a different balance for this tour. I would have liked to see him considered as additional batting cover.

### Manav Suthar

Manav Suthar's omission is another talking point. In India's previous away Test series against Sri Lanka, he took **16 wickets in four innings at an average of 21**, according to the figures provided for this analysis. That is a strong return and a reason to consider whether he deserved a place in the squad.

His left-arm spin could have offered a different option from the bowlers selected. With Jadeja and Kuldeep in the squad, the selectors have backed their existing spin options, but Suthar's omission is still worth discussing. A young player who has produced those numbers could arguably have been given a longer run at this level.

Former India spinner Ravichandran Ashwin also questioned Suthar's omission, pointing to the young left-arm spinner's strong returns and suggesting he could have been considered for the tour. That adds weight to the debate over whether India should have taken another specialist spin option.

### Should India have selected another pace all-rounder?

Nitish Kumar Reddy is the squad's main seam-bowling all-round option. An additional pace all-rounder could have provided cover if the team wanted to preserve its balance through injuries or workload management. India do have other pace options, including Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj, but they do not provide exactly the same batting balance as an all-rounder.

## My predicted bowling combination

I expect India to consider **Jasprit Bumrah, Mohammed Siraj, Prasidh Krishna and Nitish Kumar Reddy**, with Ravindra Jadeja handling the main spin role. In this setup, Nitish would provide the fourth seam option while also adding batting depth.

This is my prediction, not a confirmed playing XI. The pitch, fitness updates and the team's assessment of Bumrah's workload will determine the final combination. Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj are available as backup pace options, though they have less experience at this level.

## Final verdict: can India win in New Zealand?

India have the batting quality to compete, but the conditions will test their technique and patience. Jaiswal's two England centuries and 161 against Australia, Padikkal's 328 runs at 109.33, and Jurel's 173 at 86.50 show the potential in this batting group. Pant's ranking at No. 7 in the ICC Test batting list referenced above underlines his importance, provided he is fit to play.

With the ball, Bumrah's 14 wickets in three England Tests and 32 in five Tests against Australia show his match-winning potential. Siraj's 43 wickets in 10 Tests across those two overseas tours make him another major asset. The support bowlers must provide control so the senior quicks are not overworked.

My expected combination is three specialist pacers, Nitish Kumar Reddy as the seam-bowling all-rounder and Jadeja as the spin-bowling all-rounder, with the final selection depending on the pitch and team balance. It is a promising squad, but India will need disciplined batting, smart workload management and consistent support from the less experienced bowlers to win in New Zealand.



**My Playing XI:** KL Rahul, Yashasvi Jaiswal, Devdutt Padikkal, Shubman Gill (C), Rishabh Pant, Dhruv Jurel, Ravindra Jadeja, Nitish Kumar Reddy, Jasprit Bumrah, Mohammed Siraj, Prasidh Krishna.
