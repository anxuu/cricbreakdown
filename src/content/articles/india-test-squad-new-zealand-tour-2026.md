India are heading into another overseas Test challenge after getting back on track following the home-series defeat to South Africa, which included a heavy 408-run loss. The conditions in New Zealand will be a different test: Wellington and Christchurch can offer seam movement and assistance for fast bowlers, so India's success will depend on how well the batters handle the new ball and how effectively the pace attack uses the conditions.

The squad has a blend of established performers and less experienced options. Shubman Gill will lead the side, KL Rahul is vice-captain, and the return of Jasprit Bumrah adds quality to the pace group. Rishabh Pant's availability, however, remains subject to fitness clearance.

Let's look at the squad, assess the batting and bowling combinations, and consider the key selection questions before the two-Test series.

## Batting analysis: can India's top order adapt overseas?

India's recent batting performances in home conditions and the away series in Sri Lanka have offered encouragement. But New Zealand is a different challenge. If the pitch offers swing and seam, the top order will need patience, strong judgement outside off stump and the ability to leave the ball well.

### Yashasvi Jaiswal: a big overseas test

Jaiswal has already shown that he can make an impact in overseas conditions. In the five-Test series in England, he scored two centuries, including one in the opening Test and another in the final match. He also produced a major innings against Australia, showing the ceiling of his game when he gets set.

The challenge in New Zealand will be to turn that potential into consistent starts. If Jaiswal can see off the first spell and make the most of scoring opportunities, he can put pressure back on the hosts.

### Shubman Gill: captaincy and preparation

Gill was Player of the Series during India's previous overseas Test tour of England. India will need that quality again, both with the bat and in the way he leads the team.

Reports ahead of the tour say Gill plans to take four or five personal net bowlers to New Zealand for red-ball preparation, paying their travel and stay costs himself. He is also expected to play a Ranji Trophy match for Punjab against Chhattisgarh before travelling. The extra preparation could help him get used to the conditions, but the real test will come when New Zealand's bowlers challenge him with the new ball.

### KL Rahul and Devdutt Padikkal: important stability

Rahul and Padikkal were among India's notable performers in the previous Test series against Sri Lanka. Padikkal's reported return of 328 runs in three innings at an average of 109.33 was particularly impressive. Rahul's experience at the top and in the middle order gives India another batter who can adapt to changing match situations.

India's first three batting positions look strong on paper. The key question is whether they can carry their form into conditions where the ball may move for longer periods.

### Rishabh Pant could be a major factor

For me, Pant could be one of India's most important players if he receives fitness clearance. He has a proven ability to change the pace of a Test innings and put pressure on the opposition, including in overseas conditions. His aggressive approach can be especially valuable after the top order has spent time negotiating the seam movement.

Pant's selection is subject to fitness clearance, so India will need to be prepared with Dhruv Jurel as another wicketkeeping option. Gill, Jaiswal and Pant are all capable of influencing a match, but India's final batting balance will depend on availability and conditions.

## Bowling analysis: should India play three specialist pacers?

My preferred combination would be three specialist fast bowlers, a seam-bowling all-rounder and a spinner. That could give India a pace group built around Bumrah, Siraj and Prasidh Krishna, with Nitish Kumar Reddy as the fourth seam option and Ravindra Jadeja providing spin and batting depth. The final selection will depend on the surface, player fitness and the workload the team expects from each bowler.

### Jasprit Bumrah: quality, with workload management to consider

Bumrah's ability is not in question. His record in overseas Tests, including a strong wicket haul in Australia and an impact during the England series, shows how much he can contribute when available. He can challenge batters with pace, movement and control, and his presence gives India a genuine match-winner.

The concern is workload and availability across a two-Test series. India will need to manage him carefully rather than assume he can carry every spell at full intensity. His recent Test availability has been affected by fitness and workload considerations, so the team should have a clear plan for his spells and recovery.

### Mohammed Siraj: a key bowler in seam-friendly conditions

Siraj's energy and ability to keep running in make him a major option overseas. His tours of England and Australia have demonstrated that he can handle long spells and create pressure even when wickets do not come immediately.

In New Zealand, Siraj's discipline will matter as much as his aggression. If he can maintain a tight line, attack the top of off stump and make batters play regularly, he can help Bumrah and the other seamers build pressure from both ends.

### Prasidh Krishna and Nitish Kumar Reddy: opportunities and questions

Prasidh offers height and bounce, which could be useful on New Zealand pitches. Nitish gives the team a seam-bowling all-round option and could help balance the XI. Both, however, have less experience than Bumrah and Siraj in carrying the attack through a demanding overseas Test series.

Their control will be crucial. If they can keep the scoring rate in check while offering movement, India can maintain pressure without overworking the senior quicks.

### What about Jadeja and the other bowling options?

Jadeja may not be the first-choice wicket-taking option if the conditions strongly favour pace, especially early in the match. Still, his value should not be judged only by wickets. He can control an end, contribute with the bat and become more important as a Test progresses and the surface changes.

Kuldeep Yadav and Washington Sundar give India further spin options. Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj add pace depth, although selecting the right combination will be a balance between experience, current form and the conditions on the day.

## Notable omissions: Sarfaraz Khan and Manav Suthar

### Sarfaraz Khan

Sarfaraz's omission may surprise fans who want another proven domestic run-scorer in the middle order. His selection could have offered another batting option, but the squad already includes several specialist batters and wicketkeeper-batters. The selectors have chosen a different balance for this tour.

### Manav Suthar

Manav Suthar's omission is another talking point. He impressed during India's previous away Test series in Sri Lanka, and his left-arm spin could have offered a different option from the bowlers selected.

Ravichandran Ashwin has also questioned the decision to leave Suthar out, arguing that India may have missed an opportunity to include another specialist spinner and build depth for the future. With Jadeja and Kuldeep in the squad, the selectors have backed their existing spin options, but Suthar's omission is still worth discussing.

### Should India have selected another pace all-rounder?

Nitish Kumar Reddy is the squad's main seam-bowling all-round option. An additional pace all-rounder could have provided cover if the team wanted to preserve its balance through injuries or workload management. India do have other pace options, including Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj, but they do not provide exactly the same batting balance as an all-rounder.

## Final verdict: can India win in New Zealand?

India have the batting quality to compete, but the conditions will test their technique and patience. Jaiswal, Gill and Rahul need to set a strong foundation, while Pant could be the difference-maker if he is fit. With the ball, Bumrah and Siraj bring experience and quality, and the support bowlers must provide control so the senior quicks are not overworked.

My expected combination is three specialist pacers, Nitish Kumar Reddy as the seam-bowling all-rounder and Jadeja as the spin-bowling all-rounder, with the final place depending on the pitch and team balance. It is a promising squad, but India will need disciplined batting, smart workload management and consistent support from the less experienced bowlers to win in New Zealand.
