For eighteen seasons, Royal Challengers Bengaluru were the IPL's great unfinished sentence. Three final defeats, a graveyard of near-misses, and a fan base that had learned to brace for heartbreak before it arrived. The 2025 season broke that pattern — and the breakdown of *how* is more interesting than the trophy photo suggests.

## The problem RCB actually had

It wasn't batting. RCB had rarely lacked firepower at the top of the order across their history. The recurring failure point, year after year, was the middle overs on both sides of the ball — a soft underbelly between overs 7 and 15 that opponents targeted relentlessly.

## What changed in the death overs

The 2025 squad addressed this directly, with a bowling unit built specifically to close out tight totals rather than simply chase wickets in the powerplay. The result was a side that could defend totals in the 165–175 range consistently, something earlier RCB teams struggled to do.

## The turning point

Most title runs have one game that quietly decides everything, well before the final. For RCB, the shift in belief traces back to a mid-season chase against a top-two side, where the middle order held its nerve in a way that previous RCB line-ups hadn't. It wasn't the most statistically dramatic match of the season, but it changed how the dressing room approached tight finishes for the rest of the campaign.

## What this means going forward

The pressure is different now. RCB spent nearly two decades being defined by the wait — that story is over, and the 2026 squad will be judged on a new question: can they defend it? Early indicators from the retained squad suggest the core of the death-overs plan stays intact, which is the more important signal than any single player addition.
