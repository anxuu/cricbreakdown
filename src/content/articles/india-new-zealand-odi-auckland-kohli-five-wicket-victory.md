## Match Overview

- **Match:** India vs New Zealand
- **Format:** ODI
- **Venue:** Auckland
- **Toss:** India won the toss and elected to bowl
- **New Zealand:** 185 all out
- **India:** 186/5
- **Result:** India won by 5 wickets
- **Player of the Match:** Anshul Kamboj, 2/22 (10 overs)

## New Zealand's Innings: 185 All Out

New Zealand struggled to build sustained partnerships against India's disciplined bowling attack. Despite contributions from Tom Seifert, Daryl Mitchell and Glenn Phillips, the hosts were unable to establish a strong platform.

### Batting Highlights

- **Tom Seifert:** 38 off 43 balls
- **Glenn Phillips:** 30 off 51 balls
- **Daryl Mitchell:** 28 off 41 balls
- **Blair Tickner:** 34 off 23 balls
- **Jacob Duffy:** 15 off 21 balls

Seifert provided an important contribution at the top, while Mitchell and Phillips attempted to stabilise the innings. However, the middle order failed to convert starts into substantial scores.

Blair Tickner's late contribution of 34 off 23 balls helped New Zealand reach 185, providing some momentum towards the end of the innings.

## India's Bowling Performance

Anshul Kamboj was the standout performer with the ball, delivering an outstanding spell of 10 overs, 22 runs and two wickets.

## India's Chase: Kohli Anchors the Victory

India began their chase poorly, losing Rohit Sharma early without scoring. However, Virat Kohli took responsibility and ensured the early setback did not affect India's momentum.

| Batter | Runs | Balls |

Kohli's 74 off 51 balls was the defining contribution of the innings. His ability to combine controlled batting with attacking intent ensured India remained comfortably ahead of the required run rate.

Gill contributed 31 runs, providing support during the middle overs. Shreyas Iyer's quick 15 helped maintain momentum before KL Rahul and Naman completed the chase.

India eventually reached 186/5, completing the victory with five wickets remaining.

## Match Turning Points

### 1. Anshul Kamboj's Economical Spell

Kamboj's 2/22 in ten overs was arguably the most influential bowling performance of the match. His control over scoring prevented New Zealand from accelerating and forced the batters to take greater risks against other bowlers.

### 2. New Zealand's Middle-Order Struggles

The inability of Mitchell, Phillips and the lower middle order to build a substantial partnership meant New Zealand never gained complete control of their innings.

### 3. Virat Kohli's Aggressive Approach

Kohli's 74 off 51 balls transformed India's chase. Instead of allowing New Zealand to create pressure after Rohit's dismissal, he took control and kept India ahead of the required rate.

## My POV: What Decided the Match?

New Zealand's biggest problem was their inability to build a strong middle-order partnership. Although Tickner provided a late contribution, 185 was not a sufficiently challenging total against India's batting lineup.

India's bowling discipline, particularly Kamboj's economical spell, created the foundation for the victory. The chase was then controlled by Kohli, whose innings ensured India never lost complete control of the game.

If New Zealand had managed another 40–50 runs through a middle-order partnership, the contest could have become significantly more competitive.

## Player of the Match: Anshul Kamboj

Anshul Kamboj deserved the Player of the Match recognition for his outstanding bowling performance of 2/22 in ten overs.

His economical spell restricted New Zealand's scoring and created pressure throughout the innings. While Kohli's batting was equally important in the chase, Kamboj's contribution helped establish the conditions for India's comfortable victory.

## Final Verdict

India dominated the match through disciplined bowling and a controlled batting chase. Anshul Kamboj's economical spell and Virat Kohli's attacking 74 proved decisive.

New Zealand will need greater middle-order consistency and stronger partnerships if they are to challenge India in the remaining matches.
