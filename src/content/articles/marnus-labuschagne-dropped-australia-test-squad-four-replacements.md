Australian cricket has witnessed a major selection development, with Marnus Labuschagne dropped from Australia's plans for the upcoming four-Test series against New Zealand.

Chairman of selectors George Bailey confirmed on November 9 that the 32-year-old would not be considered for the series, beginning December 9 in Perth.

The decision marks the second time in 17 months that Labuschagne has lost his place in Australia's Test setup.

Once regarded as one of the most dependable batters in world cricket, the former ICC No. 1 Test batter has struggled to reproduce the consistency that made him a vital part of Australia's middle order.

Bailey explained that the decision would give Labuschagne some space away from the constant speculation surrounding his position while allowing selectors to explore alternative batting combinations.

## Why Was Marnus Labuschagne Dropped from Australia's Test Team?

Labuschagne's prolonged decline in Test cricket has been the biggest reason behind his omission.

His struggles continued during Australia's recent three-Test tour of South Africa, where he managed just 131 runs in six innings at an average of 21.83.

His performance in the series-deciding Test was particularly disappointing, registering scores of zero and four as Australia lost the match and the series 2-1.

Another concerning statistic is that Labuschagne has gone 49 Test innings without scoring a century. His last Test hundred came against England at Old Trafford during the 2023 Ashes.

His inability to convert promising starts into substantial scores has eventually forced Australia's selectors to reconsider his place in the batting lineup.

## Marnus Labuschagne Test Stats Before and After 2023: What Went Wrong?

Perhaps the most striking aspect of Labuschagne's downfall is the contrast between his performances during his peak years and his recent returns.

Between 2019 and 2022, Labuschagne established himself as one of the most dominant batters in Test cricket.

He scored 2,990 runs across those four calendar years, averaging above 56 in each year. His consistency helped him reach the No. 1 position in the ICC Test batting rankings in 2021.

However, his performances declined considerably from 2023 onwards.

His career average, which stood at 59.43 after his first 33 Tests, has now fallen to 42.64.

The contrast explains why Australia's selectors have finally decided to look beyond their former No. 3 batter.

## Marnus Labuschagne Comeback Plans: Can He Fight His Way Back?

Despite being dropped, Labuschagne has made it clear that he has no intention of giving up on his international career.

Speaking after the selection announcement, he expressed confidence about returning to the Australian Test team.

> "I've got no doubt I will be back there. When? Who knows."

Labuschagne will now return to domestic cricket, where he will captain Queensland in the Sheffield Shield.

Interestingly, he has already experienced a similar situation. Following his omission from Australia's 2025 West Indies tour, he responded with consecutive Sheffield Shield centuries, scoring 160 against Tasmania and 159 against South Australia.

Those performances helped him earn his place back in the Australian side.

## Sheffield Shield 2026/27: Performances That Could Decide Australia's Next No. 3

The upcoming Sheffield Shield matches will be crucial in determining Australia's next Test batting combination.

Labuschagne's omission has created an opportunity for other domestic performers to push their case for international selection.

Interestingly, Labuschagne himself has acknowledged the quality of the available replacements, specifically mentioning Jack Clayton, Sam Konstas and Kurtis Patterson.

He has also expressed his willingness to support Clayton if the Queensland youngster receives an opportunity to represent Australia.

Australia's selectors are expected to finalise their New Zealand squad after the fourth round of Sheffield Shield matches.

## Can Marnus Labuschagne Return to Australia's Test Team in 2027?

Although Labuschagne has been ruled out of the New Zealand series, his international career is far from over.

George Bailey has indicated that a return remains possible, with Australia's upcoming tours of India and England in 2027 providing potential opportunities.

For Labuschagne, the challenge is straightforward: score runs, regain consistency and remind selectors of the qualities that once made him one of the world's best Test batters.

His previous comeback demonstrates that he can respond to setbacks. However, this time the competition for his position is likely to be intense.

## Marnus Labuschagne's Future: The Fall of Australia's Former No. 1 Test Batter

Marnus Labuschagne's situation represents one of the most interesting selection stories in Australian cricket.

From averaging nearly 60 during the early stages of his Test career to seeing his overall average fall to 42.64, his decline has been significant.

His exclusion has also created an opportunity for four different contenders to compete for Australia's No. 3 position.

Whether Australia invest in the youth of Sam Konstas, the experience of Kurtis Patterson, the international exposure of Nathan McSweeney or the domestic form of Jack Clayton remains to be seen.

For Labuschagne, the next few months could determine whether this becomes another successful comeback story or a defining turning point in his international career.

The former No. 1 is down, but is he out? Australia's next selection decision could decide the future of both Marnus Labuschagne and the next generation of Australian Test batting.
